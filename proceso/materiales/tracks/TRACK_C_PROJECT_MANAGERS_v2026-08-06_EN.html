1. Profile and mission

The PM is the owner of on-site execution. He does not swing the hammer — he manages the subcontractors who do. His mission: "make the project actually move" — the way it was planned in the schedule (THE KEY). He coordinates every trade, verifies the standard, documents in the field, and keeps the schedule glued to reality so the job advances along its critical path and passes city inspection on the first attempt.

The 2 core principles (JD §1.1–1.2):
1. Ownership: every task on the schedule is the PM's responsibility even when the PM is not the actor. A "it didn't get done and nobody said why" never exists on a PM's job. Own the outcome, not the excuse.
2. The client ALWAYS hears it first from the PM — with the problem + a proposed path, never just the problem.

The mindset (the 5 behaviors — Jose 2026-08-04): failures don't come from the trade or the theory — they come from behavior: rigor · attention to detail · foresee · solve · relentless proactivity. The track's mental rule: "do not accept the problem and react: prevent it, or hunt for solutions relentlessly until the schedule is met, whatever it takes." The material, the sub and the inspection are foreseen BEFORE they blow up — not discovered on EXECUTE day. This behavior is evaluated (§4).

3. Modules (8 sessions)

Format: recorded session (for review) + live practice on real projects + homework for the 2:30 meeting. Each module has its HTML deck (05_MATERIALES/presentaciones/pm/), its video script (05_MATERIALES/guiones_video/pm.md, EN section) and closes with at least one real case from the case book.

MODULE C1 — The role: THE KEY, ownership and the 10 Commandments (~60 min)

Objective: the PM explains without reading what is expected of him, recites the 10 Commandments applied to a case, and names the 5 behaviors.

Content:
- The declared problem #1 (P1) and the company's answer. The system is not bureaucracy: it is the PM's scaffold.
- THE KEY (canon §1): CNG lays no bricks — it subcontracts everything. MANAGE THE JOB THE WAY IT WAS PLANNED IN THE SCHEDULE: impeccable information flow + solve the unforeseen without leaving the schedule. The margin is earned managing the schedule, not executing work.
- The 5 behaviors (the root cause, Jose translated): "there is no rigor, no attention to detail, we don't foresee and we don't solve, we are not proactive; we accept problems and react, instead of preventing them or hunting for solutions relentlessly to meet the schedule whatever it takes." → rigor · attention to detail · foresee · solve · relentless proactivity.
- Ownership: if a sub no-shows, the PM doesn't report "he didn't come" and close the day — he finds out why, re-dates with a reason, lines up the recovery and tells the system.
- Client first: proactive, never chased; problem + proposed path.
- The 10 Commandments, one by one (JD §8). Memorize: II (no work starts without a SIGNED WO), IV (confirm twice), VII (material before the crew), VIII (city last), IX (bill+payment+lien waiver+Gantt 100%).
- What the PM does NOT do (JD §3): doesn't create tasks, doesn't carry money, doesn't set strategy. Coordinates, verifies, documents, un-sticks.
- Values (JD §7): respect on EVERY channel; NO yelling, NO insults — ever.
- The model success case (P3): the ex-salesperson PM who quoted everything and negotiated prices BEFORE starting, everything with a WO → the FASTEST project. "If people follow the process, their job will go well, because it is a good process."

Practice on a real case: open one of the PM's own projects in JobTread [VERIFY WITH JOSE: current project roster per PM]. Walk the schedule and point out live: where am I violating a Commandment today? Written list of 3 own violations and their fix.

The real case (case book #6 + Charles Harris): unsigned WO / after-the-fact WO — the sub who claimed his payment 1 day after finishing and by contract had to wait (his signed WO said "1 week after"); and Martha 24-Jul: "urgent: create the work order for Cesar" — the WO AFTER the work. And Charles Harris: 24 straight days without one photo, with a bank draw at stake — without ownership, the job goes invisible.

What is evaluated: recites the 10 Commandments with an own case each · names the 5 behaviors and gives a real "prevent vs react" example from his own job.

MODULE C2 — The tools and the PM's clock (~60 min)

Objective: master the daily mechanics: JobTread (visibility, not authorship), CompanyCam, KITBOT, the 2:30 meeting and the reports he receives.

Content:
- JobTread: the spine. Duties: clock in on arrival / clock out on departure, honest status (done / not done / moved), re-date WITH a reason. The PM does NOT create tasks — Backoffice does, decided in the meeting.
- The daily meeting: 2:30 PM (OFFICIAL time, Jose 2026-08-06), Mon–Fri, ~2 h. Jose calls the PMs; each gives his progress; the schedules of ALL projects are re-dated. "That is where the real weight of the work is." The meeting recording/transcript IS FED TO THE SYSTEM EVERY DAY — what the PM says in the meeting is on the record and the system uses it ("what was said rules over what was scheduled"). The PM arrives with the reality of EVERY job, not impressions.
- "After 3 PM, JobTread is the truth of the day." If it's not in JT after the meeting, it didn't happen.
- CompanyCam: photos mandatory WITH a description on every job worked today. "A photo without context is not documentation." Documentation is the signal that releases payment.
- KITBOT today: answers expert-level technical questions (codes, inspection requirements, sequencing — ask the bot, don't guess) and sends the Task List at 8:00 PM Mon–Fri: TOMORROW geared to the process + assignments/reminders + the 5/3/1 inspection rule. ⚠️ Real state: the check-ins, the 4 PM pre-close alert and "Your Day Tomorrow" no longer run (Jose's decision 06-Aug) — the PM's day plan is his Task List from last night; evaluation is weekly. [VERIFY WITH JOSE: current daily verification mechanism for the Día Cumplido while pre-close alerts are off.]
- The PM's clock today: last night's Task List → clock in + walk the site before the crew (Commandment V) → job + documentation → 2:30 PM meeting → close the day with today's tasks executed or re-dated with a reason → 8:00 PM tomorrow's Task List arrives.
- The 6 Día Cumplido checks (see §4 — they are the certification).
- Schedule naming: <TRADE>: <ACTION> (SHEETROCK: SELECT VENDOR & SEND WO). Old projects use old names; the current standard is the official template's.

Practice on a real case: full simulated day: (1) read last night's Task List and build the plan; (2) clock in; (3) upload 3 real photos to CompanyCam with useful descriptions (what, where, state); (4) in the real 2:30 meeting, report every task of today with status + reason; (5) verify everything said landed in JT after the meeting.

MODULE C3 — The construction process and the official MASTER TEMPLATE (~90 min)

Objective: the PM masters the physical critical path, navigates the official template and knows exactly which inspection is requested when — and what does NOT get scheduled until which one passes.

Content:
- 📌 The official MASTER TEMPLATE is the source (TEMPLATE_MAESTRO_JOBTREAD_2026-08-06.csv, ingested 2026-08-06): 34 trade groups · 411 tasks (209 PM Tasks · 64 Purchases · 40 Critical Path · 14 Action · 45 Payment-Subs · 21 Payment-Materials · 18 City Inspections). The official file RULES over any reconstruction. The 34 groups in order: FOUNDATION · 811 · IN GROUND ELECTRICAL · IN GROUND PLUMBING · FRAMING · DOORS AND WINDOWS · ROOFING · PROJECT CLEANING - FRAMING · ELECTRICAL/MECHANICAL/PLUMBING ROUGH IN · INSULATION · SHEETROCK · PROJECT CLEANING - SHEETROCK · INTERIOR PAINT · FLOORING · TILES · GLASS · INTERIOR DOORS · BASEBOARDS AND TRIM · CABINETS · COUNTERTOPS · ELECTRICAL/MECHANICAL/PLUMBING TRIM · BRICKS · SIDING · EXTERIOR PAINT · GUTTERS · GARAGE DOOR · PRE DELIVERY WALK THROUGH · FINAL PUNCH LIST · PROJECT DELIVERY · FINAL INSPECTIONS.
- The physical sequence and its logic (canon §2): MEP In-Ground → Foundation (Formboard → Survey → city Report → pour → ~10 days curing) → Framing → Sheathing + Roof Decking → exterior doors/windows → MEP Rough-Ins (+inspection of EACH) → Framing Inspection → interior (priority) / exterior in parallel. Interior: Zip Tape → Insulation → Sheetrock (hang → tape, bed & texture) → Primer (final paint goes LAST) → Flooring & Tiles → glass → carpentry → Cabinets → Countertops → MEP Trims (+inspection) → final paint → Pre-Delivery Walk → punch list → cleaning → Final Inspection (THE CITY) → delivery.
- (P1, translated): "one thing keeps blocking the next… that line of dependencies becomes the critical path because it is what cannot be compressed any further." In a real schedule, unsold trades are removed — the template is the maximum.
- Per-trade structure in the template (real example, SHEETROCK, in the official CSV's order): REVIEW PORTFOLIO & CC CHECKLIST: BEFORE SOW → CREATE TAKEOFF REPORT (Purchases lane) → CONFIRM TAKEOFF DETAILS (the PM, on site) → (Purchases lane: SECURE PRICING & ETA → PURCHASE MATERIALS → DELIVERY ETA) → CREATE SOW REPORT & SOURCE VENDORS → SELECT VENDOR & SEND WO → CONFIRM SCHEDULE WITH SUB → CC CHECKLIST - PM INSPECTION (BEFORE) → INSTALL SHEETROCK → PM INSPECTION (AFTER) → DRYWALL SCREW CITY INSPECTION → TAPE, BED AND TEXTURE → PM INSPECTION (AFTER) → BILL & PAYMENT. In FRAMING the template includes DOWN PAYMENT (canon §8: the downpayment is special, only for CERTAIN subs/trades — framing, MEP rough-ins; the only pre-work payment).
- The inspection sequencing rule: rough-ins pass their inspection BEFORE anything covers them ("don't schedule insulation or drywall until MEP passes").
- The 5/3/1 rule (Task List v4, approved by Jose 2026-08-06): for every City Inspection ≤5 days away, if there is NO record the PM reviewed it with the bot, the Task List reminds him: at 5 days = offer to review the codes together · at 3 days = reminder · at 1 day = final notice ("TOMORROW is your inspection and you didn't check with me whether you'd pass"). If he ALREADY reviewed it with the bot → silence. The professional PM's move: review the inspection with KITBOT as soon as it shows in his week — the bot has all the building codes.
- Where CNG fails most (P1): MEP rough-ins (observations) and framing (outdated / non-engineer-approved plans reaching the sub → rework); paint/texture "always gets ruined on us."
- Rework case (P1): insulation foam next to a hot-water line → fire risk → redo. The Before checklist exists to catch exactly this.

Practice on a real case: on the official template (CSV) and the PM's real schedule: rebuild the dependency chain of 3 trades (INSULATION → SHEETROCK → INTERIOR PAINT), mark which city inspection releases each step, and answer: "if sheetrock is dated Monday, what 3 things do I verify BEFORE Friday?" Then: find the next City Inspection on his project and review it with KITBOT live (so 5/3/1 finds it already reviewed).

The real case (case book #3): Martha's red tags (24-Jul) — inspection rejected for wrong sequence requested and plans not present on site (the failure that had already been predicted). Two days burned by two 5-minute checks.

What is evaluated: draws the full sequence from memory with 3 "no X without Y" dependencies · explains the inspection sequence of HIS project · reached his last inspection with the 5/3/1 review done (the 1-day reminder never fires on him).

MODULE C4 — The full pre-EXECUTE chain: the golden rule (~90 min) ⭐ THE MOST IMPORTANT SESSION

Objective: the PM executes the complete preparation chain of a trade and swears the golden rule: an EXECUTE only gets a date if its whole prior chain is 100% closed.

Content (the chain, in order — EL_FLOW + official template):
1. REVIEW PORTFOLIO & CC CHECKLIST: BEFORE SOW — the Portfolio = "the PM's bible": before/after layout, what gets demolished/added, measurements (W3 → table), electrical plan. Check plans vs field BEFORE anything. The Before is reviewed TWICE (P3): at trade start (map the trade to the project) and ~3 days before work starts (double check = step 8).
2. CONFIRM TAKEOFF DETAILS — Romina is in Peru: the PM measures ON SITE and fills the report. The 3 takeoff evidences (Jose 2026-08-06): the takeoff report in CompanyCam must end up containing (1) photo of the CONFIRMED/locked price, (2) the supplier's WRITTEN ETA, (3) the RECEIPT of the purchase. They are Romina's deliverables, but the PM KNOWS them and demands them: a takeoff without the 3 evidences is a purchase cycle without a file. In framing, the takeoff/sizing is done by the lumber supplier (Purchases sends the plans); the PM supervises that it was requested and checks plans-vs-field.
3. CREATE SOW REPORT & SOURCE VENDORS — SOW as a CompanyCam report: photos of where each thing goes, measurements, exact wording (real example P2: "installation and relocation of nine windows and two doors"). Jose's brief (P3, translated): "give the sub ALL the information in a structured way so he understands the job and quotes a fair price." A vague SOW produces a wrong job (Commandment III). It is exported and sent to ≥3 subs asking: price, when he can come, and schedule a site visit. ⚠️ Provisional: the CEO approves the SOW before it goes out. Backoffice audits that the document EXISTS in CompanyCam.
4. SELECT VENDOR & SEND WO — evaluate the 3, choose, and define the WO data that Backoffice formalizes: sub → trade → budget → negotiated amount → SOW copied inside (or SOW PDF attached) → ALWAYS the Payment Breakdown. ELECTRONIC SIGNATURE flow (P3): the sub must be registered as a vendor (name, email, phone + documentation) → set as WO recipient → the system sends SMS + email → the sub SIGNS electronically → only THEN is it marked "Approved" in JT. The visible signature = legal seal — "it is not like a word-of-mouth agreement." Hard rule: NO work starts without a SIGNED WO (Commandment II).
5. CONFIRM SCHEDULE WITH SUB — call 3–5 days ahead AND confirm in writing (Commandment IV: one channel is a hope; two is a commitment). It exists "because the managers themselves forget to do things" (P2).
6. Romina's lane closed: material bought and DELIVERY ETA confirmed — material BEFORE the crew (Commandment VII). Reading the ETA: >1 day = arrives by the end date at the latest; =1 day = in stock or unverified (ask). "It's ordered" ≠ "ETA confirmed": a stale ETA "lies to the schedule."
7. (if applicable) DOWN PAYMENT — only certain subs/trades (framing, MEP rough-ins); Milagros executes it; the only pre-work payment.
8. CC CHECKLIST - PM INSPECTION (BEFORE) — walk the site BEFORE the sub: site prepared, preconditions ready, predecessor trade signed off. Not ready = no start.

The golden rule (EL_FLOW §0): if at the 2:30 meeting an EXECUTE has a date this week, the prior tasks of its group are at 100%. If not, the EXECUTE moves — it is not left "just in case."

Practice on a real case: take a REAL upcoming trade of the PM's project and run the full chain live: check the portfolio, confirm the takeoff on site (and verify the report has a path to the 3 evidences), write the SOW in CompanyCam (reviewed against the "no room for interpretation" standard), send the 3 bid requests, define the WO data with Payment Breakdown, and at 2:30 declare the EXECUTE date ONLY when the chain is 100%. Second exercise (audit): find every EXECUTE dated with a chain <100% in his own schedule and propose the re-date. Third: open one of his old WOs and audit it against the standard (scope inside? payment breakdown? signature?) — the example Jose reviewed live in P3 was WRONG (no scope).

The real case (case book #2 + #1): the week of Jun-29 produced HALF of what was projected by violating the golden rule (Martha: ELECTRICAL ROUGH IN dated Jul 7–8 with SELECT VENDOR & SEND WO and CONFIRM SCHEDULE WITH SUB at 0% — the day came and there was no electrician). And Katie Holmes' doors: framed at 8 ft when the design said 82"; on Jul-23, 96" doors were ordered = THE ERROR WAS PURCHASED. The plans-vs-field check and CONFIRM TAKEOFF DETAILS exist to catch this.

What is evaluated: draws the full chain from memory and states the golden rule · 1 real chain executed end to end with the WO SIGNED before any start · zero EXECUTEs dated with a chain <100% in his schedule.

MODULE C5 — Negotiating with the budget: the max cost and the complete WO (~60 min)

Objective: the PM uses the budget as his negotiation ceiling and closes subs for ≤ the line-item cost, with a complete, signed WO.

Content:
- Budget structure (canon §7): the signed estimate becomes the budget; line item = quantity · unit (typically sqft) · unit cost · extended cost.
- Cost vs price: price = what the client pays; cost = what CNG can pay the sub. Cost = price × (1 − margin). Normal margin = 40%. Set ONCE at estimate→budget conversion (lower if there was a discount).
- The example Jose taught (P2, Kyle Aubrey, paint): walls 3,373 sqft → cost $3,035.7 · ceiling 1,456 sqft × $1.32 → $1,921 · siding 616 sqft → $628. The literal call (translated): "I have to paint 3,373 sqft of wall, 1,456 of ceiling and 616 of siding, and for all of that I can pay you the sum of these three." — "That is how a Project Manager gets the jobs done."
- Negotiating for LESS saves for CNG (real WO example: budget $20,000 → closed at $18,000 = $2,000 saved).
- The complete WO (P3): document → work order → sub → trade → budget → negotiated amount → Scope of Work inside → Payment Breakdown ALWAYS (standard payment = the Friday after finishing; the "day after" exception is agreed explicitly; sometimes split ROUGH / PASS INSPECTION). Then: electronic signature (C4 flow) and only then "Approved."
- Who registers: Backoffice formalizes in JT, but the data comes from the PM — amount, terms, scope. Without the PM's data there is no clean WO.
- Renegotiations (P3): if the CEO/COO renegotiates a price the PM already closed, it MUST be notified so the WO is updated and we don't overpay. The PM verifies his WO reflects the latest price.
- The first filter of every decision is whether it fits the budget.

Practice on a real case: open Kyle Aubrey's real budget and reproduce Jose's calculation line by line. Then, with a pending trade of his own: extract the max cost per line item, prepare the negotiation script, simulate the call with Jose as counterpart, and fill in the WO data (amount + payment breakdown) so Backoffice formalizes it and the sub signs it.

The real case (case book #10 + amounts): ambiguous amounts in descriptions ("43k" on Rohini, "551872" on Martha) — the amount lives in the task description and the system parses it; ambiguous formats break cashflow and productivity. And Karim: empty Critical Path tasks = $0 income in productivity. What is not properly registered does not exist for the business.

What is evaluated: computes the max cost given price and margin · 1 real negotiation closed at ≤ the line-item cost · his latest WO has scope inside + payment breakdown + electronic signature before start.

MODULE C6 — Execution: live checklists, the city and the payment (~90 min)

Objective: the PM supervises execution with the checklist in hand, never calls the city early, and closes the trade through payment with complete documentation.

Content:
- During: daily photos WITH descriptions + the During section of the checklist with its HOLD POINTS: nothing gets covered (drywall, dirt, concrete) without verifying and photographing it first. P2 (translated): "every time he goes to inspect, he has to go with the checklist in his hand."
- Checklists are filled LIVE by section (Before ×2 / During / After) — the typical resistance is filling everything at the end of the trade, which defeats the purpose (P3). Backoffice audits the During: active trade without photos or entries = flag.
- Inspection on every visit (Commandment VI): every site visit ends with an inspection against the checklist + photos with descriptions.
- After: final verification, photos, lien waiver, reconciled invoice. The After unlocks the payment — "if the PM hasn't filled his report, there is no record of his inspection → we don't consider the work finished → the sub doesn't get paid."
- Commandment VIII — the city last: never call until (1) the own inspection passes AND (2) the checklist is 100%. The 5/3/1 rule is the safety net: if the PM didn't review the inspection with the bot, the system chases him at 5/3/1 days. KITBOT researches what that city checks so the job passes on the first attempt.
- The payment gate: checklist 100% of required items = the payment signal. Double lock: PM verifies on site ("work approved on site" → notifies Backoffice) + Backoffice reviews the checklist (2nd lock) → Proceed with Payment → Milagros executes with her 4 filters (in the week's plan? same amount? good position? documentation = WO + inspections?). Without the complete file, the payment fails filter 4 — the PM's documentation is literally what makes his sub payable.
- Standard payment = the Friday after finishing; sometimes split ROUGH / PASS INSPECTION (the last part only when the city passed).
- Commandment IX: task complete = bill + payment + lien waiver + Gantt 100%. "Done in the field" is not "done."
- Collection: with the trade closed and documented, the CEO requests the draw — every finished trade becomes a collection. The PM's documentation is the first link of the money coming in.
- ⚠️ Real state of checklists (canon §11): rolling out trade by trade. Where they EXIST, they are audited (Before×2, active During, After before payment); where they don't, the discipline runs on photos WITH descriptions + the PM's inspection. The gate rule is not suspended: the equivalent is documented.

Practice on a real case: on an active trade: (1) visit with checklist in hand, item by item; (2) identify the hold points and photograph BEFORE anything is covered; (3) simulate the close: "what am I missing TODAY to be able to call the city?" (exact list); (4) build the payment file: After photos + lien waiver + invoice → notify "work approved on site" and request the Backoffice review. Reverse audit: open Martha's instantiated checklists and explain what having them at 0% cost.

The real case (case book #9 + #5): Martha: 30 checklists instantiated, 29 at 0/x — instantiating is not filling; a 0% checklist releases no payment and catches no errors. And Ginger's leak: a punctured gas line was located thanks to photos from 2 years earlier the PM took on her own initiative — "to be able to find where the error was… so it doesn't happen again." The checklist institutionalizes that reflex.

What is evaluated: 1 trade supervised with the checklist live (hold points photographed) · payment file accepted by Backoffice on the first try · his last City Inspection passed on the first attempt (or the honest analysis of why not).

MODULE C7 — Money in the schedule: the 2 new rules (~60 min) ⭐ NEW

Objective: the PM registers (or gets registered) EVERY money movement in the schedule and closes tasks with squared dates — the 2 rules Jose set on the night of 2026-08-06.

Content:
- (a) ALL money goes through the schedule (canon §6 — RULE WITHOUT EXCEPTIONS): EVERY purchase is registered in the schedule even if last-minute, with its type (Payment - Materials / Payment - Subs). And EVERY expense — even Home Depot or petty cash — is recorded to the project as an Additional Expense. If money moved and there is no task in the schedule, the record is incomplete: cashflow, productivity and the reports go blind. The PM's role: he doesn't create the tasks, but he is the source: every field expense is reported THAT day (bot / meeting / Backoffice) so its task + its Expense exist.
- (b) RULES FOR MOVING THE SCHEDULE (canon §4): the schedule is the reflection of what happens or happened in reality, and the OPTIMISTIC projection of what will happen. When closing a task the dates must be SQUARED — the check is not enough: a check on a date that isn't when it really happened is a dirty record (it breaks Friday-vs-Friday, cashflow and productivity). Closing = check + real dates squared.
- The payment nuance (canon §8): on payment tasks, the original date = the contractual trigger (completion + terms) and is NOT rewritten; the payment MOVES and is recorded on the date it was REALLY paid. Squaring dates applies to job reality; the contractual trigger is preserved.
- Schedule checks = CONFIRMED/reserved payments (they happen in the week no matter what; if funds fall short, they roll with priority). The source of truth for confirmed payments is the dashboard's Finance tab — not the schedule checks.
- The weekly money cycle (canon §9.2): FRIDAY cashflow meeting → PLAN OF THE WEEK (budget per project) → the CEO approves. Milagros notifies the group of every payment made. The PM follows up with his Your Next Week (his confirmed payments/purchases per project).
- Why this matters to the PM: the amount lives in the task description (the system parses it — clear numbers, no minus sign); his productivity is computed from Action/CP; his weekly evaluation is computed from the schedule. Dirty record = dirty evaluation.
- Subs are never paid up front ("they leave and strand you") — exception: the downpayment of certain trades (C4).

Practice on a real case: (1) take 3 recent real expenses from his job (including one from Home Depot / petty cash) and trace: does its schedule task and its Expense exist? — report today the ones missing; (2) review his last 5 checks: do the dates match when it really happened? — propose the squaring; (3) find a moved payment task in his schedule and explain why the original date is NOT rewritten.
