ROLE CARD — COO (the ficha)

Mission: make the plan and reality be the same thing. The schedule states a theory; the COO lives the ground and guarantees they match — and when they don't, he corrects or escalates before the critical path breaks. North star: no job leaves its critical path; quality is never negotiated.
Owns Schedule vs reality · quality in ALL jobs · the critical path · on-site resolution
His money lane (canon §8) New Negotiations WITH DETAILS → cashflow data · Request Payments → Administrator's 4 filters · renegotiations ALWAYS notified (update the WO) · Update Schedule after a payment unblocks work
Receives The 4 management reports + 2 live dashboards · field reality · flags >$1,000 and pre-negative (with the CEO) · PM escalations
Delivers Negotiations with details · verified schedule-vs-reality · protected critical path · his site trail (CC photos/dictated notes)
Does NOT Carry the money (Administrator) · face the client / commercial strategy (CEO) · do the PM's job (he directs, verifies, unblocks)
Día Cumplido 1) ≥1 site visited WITH trail · 2) schedule-vs-reality of it confirmed/corrected at 2:30 PM · 3) zero payments in his name overdue without action/hand-off · 4) ≥1 system signal answered (source ROLE_coo.md — official file still says "pendiente de definir" → [VERIFICAR CON JOSE])
Behavior The 5: rigor · attention to detail · foresee · solve · insatiable proactivity — never accept-and-react

MODULE E1 · The COO seat — plan = reality (the role in the process)

Objective: explain in 2 minutes what the COO owns, what he never does, and why his seat exists in THE KEY of CNG.

Content:
- THE KEY (canon §1): manage the work the way it was planned in the schedule — the margin (~40%) is earned managing the schedule, not executing the work. CNG is a GC: it subcontracts every trade; its core is impeccable management of schedule, budgets, hires.
- The COO's translation: he is the human guarantee that "JobTread is a mirror of what is happening in reality" (Jose, P3) holds true ON SITE. PMs feed the mirror; the Backoffice records it; the COO verifies it against the ground, one level above the Lead PM the designed org had.
- The four ownerships (roles 2026-07-03): schedule vs reality · quality · critical path · on-site resolution. Plus the trail rule: a visit that leaves no photos/notes did not happen for the system.
- The boundaries (what he does NOT do): money = Administrator · client/strategy = CEO · daily execution = PM. The COO who starts doing the PM's job stops directing the operation.
- The behavior root cause (Jose 2026-08-04, verbatim): "no se tiene rigurosidad, no se tiene atención al detalle, no se prevé y no se soluciona, no se es proactivo; aceptamos los problemas y reaccionamos…" — the COO models the 5 for everyone.

Real case: GUIA_CEO ficha Rey (2026-07-03): as of that audit — CERO COO mentions in the 11 bitácoras, a payment 21 days overdue (Rhodes Surveyor), ~54 pings unanswered in 18 days. The role's own starting point IS the case study: the instinct was right, the trail was missing. The example comes from the top (§6.5).

Exercise: pick 2 active projects; for each, say out loud: where is its critical path today, when was your last visit with a trail, and what would you check tomorrow on site to confirm the schedule is telling the truth.

Evaluated: states mission + 4 ownerships + 3 boundaries without notes; his last 5 site visits all have a CompanyCam/bot trail.

MODULE E2 · Reading the 4 management reports — and what each one decides

Objective: read the official management reporting in ≤20 min/day and turn each report into ONE kind of decision — never into recalculation.

Content — the 4 reports (canon §9.4, all with Rey as audience) + 2 dashboards:

Report When What the COO DECIDES from it
Daily Report (gerencia) 8:00 PM Mon–Fri Tomorrow's route: which site he visits, which 🔴 traba he unblocks, what "gerencia debe responder" is HIS to answer. Format: narrative summary + day's photos + verdict ✅/🔴 + "¿qué sucede mañana?"
Project Department Weekly Evaluation Sat 8:30 AM Where the week really broke: gantts (Action/CP/City only) + paid/collected (confirmed vs non-confirmed-executed) + each PM's metrics + % AI adoption. Decides: which PM needs correction, which chain gets his week's attention, what he raises Friday.
Project Department Next Week Report On-demand (after cashflow consolidates) His week plan: pure plan view + ONLY confirmed money + approved payments per project + milestone movement. Decides: which visits align to the critical path and to this week's collections.
Monthly Gerencial Day 1, 8:00 AM Structural drift: initial baseline vs last Friday, works only (Action + CP + collections). Decides: which project needs a reset conversation, not a patch.
Productividad (dashboard) live, every 20 min Whether net production covers the fixed overhead — the operation's oxygen.
Carrera (dashboard) live + 7:35 AM analysis Month pace vs baseline (Jonas reviews it; the COO uses it for pace pressure).
  • The reporting laws (canon §9.5) — the COO obeys them like everyone: (0) baseline = Friday 11:00 PM; GRAY = the Friday photo, GREEN = what really happened · (1) evaluation = closes Friday vs Friday, never "the schedule didn't move" · (2) the official report's numbers RULE — no side recalculation · (3) "what was said rules over what was scheduled" · (4) after 3 PM, JobTread is the truth of the day.
  • The honest algorithm (PM Weekly Evaluation): % = only tasks finished ON TIME; extended = orange + ››› arrows. Read the PMs' weeklies knowing the number is already honest — do not soften it, do not harshen it.
  • Style law C2: reschedules are presented in neutral, without blame — they are the schedule reflecting reality.

Real case: case #2 of the case book — the week of 29-jun produced HALF of projection because EXECUTEs were dated with their chain at 0% (Martha: ELECTRICAL ROUGH IN dated 7–8 jul with SELECT VENDOR & SEND WO and CONFIRM SCHEDULE at 0%). A COO reading the Next Week report against the golden rule catches this BEFORE the week starts.

Exercise: take the latest real Daily Report + Project Dept Weekly. Extract: 3 decisions (visit / unblock / correct), 1 thing that is NOT yours (route it: money → Administrator, client → CEO), and 1 number you were tempted to recalculate — and didn't.

Evaluated: given a real report set, produces the 3 decisions in <15 min; never quotes a number different from the official one; never phrases a reschedule as blame.

MODULE E3 · Renegotiations — with details, always notified

Objective: run every negotiation so the cashflow sees it and the company never overpays.

Content:
- His lane (canon §8): New Negotiations → the terms go to the system WITH DETAILS — amounts, dates, conditions. That input (plus the Administrator's Friday balances and the Backoffice's Daily Schedule Update) builds the CASHFLOW DATA → CASHFLOW PLAN → CEO approves.
- The renegotiation rule (canon §8, P3): if CEO/COO renegotiates a price already closed by the PM (e.g. $1,000 → $800) he MUST notify the change so the WO is updated — otherwise the payment goes out at the old amount. The Administrator is gatekeeper of every negotiation: a price change that never reached her = money overpaid.
- His Request Payments face the 4 filters (in the week's plan? · same amount? · good financial position? · documentation = SIGNED WO + inspections). A COO request without a signed WO does not pass — and should not.
- Money rules he negotiates within: subs are never paid up front ("se van y te dejan varado" — Jose); standard payment = the Friday after finishing; downpayment = exception for CERTAIN subs/trades (framing, MEP rough-ins) with its gate. The payment task's original date = contractual trigger — it is never rewritten; the payment MOVES and is recorded on the real pay date.
- All money passes through the schedule (canon §6): a negotiation that moved money with no task in the schedule leaves cashflow, productivity and reports blind.

Real cases: case #6 — a sub claimed payment 1 day after finishing; his signed WO said "1 week after" and by contract he waited: the WO's terms are the negotiation, in writing. And case #7 (Felton, 17-jul) — a bounced client check conditioning whether the sub works Saturday: when money touches execution, it escalates THAT day.

Exercise: simulate renegotiating a closed price down on a real project (Karim or Martha). Write the 3-line notification: old price → new price · which WO gets updated · what the cashflow gains. Then run your last real Request Payment through the 4 filters out loud.

Evaluated: zero renegotiations without same-day notification; every negotiation delivered WITH details (amount, date, terms); zero payment requests without their signed WO.

MODULE E4 · Critical path & collection milestones — the money path

Objective: protect the critical path as the company's collection engine: each finished trade becomes a collection.

Content:
- The physical critical path (canon §2): the staggered template (MEP in-ground → foundation → framing → … → trims → final). "Una cosa te va bloqueando la otra" — it stretches, never shrinks. Blue = critical; the interior chain is the longest. The official template: 34 trade groups · 411 tasks (TEMPLATE_MAESTRO_JOBTREAD_2026-08-06.csv — the official file RULES).
- The golden rule (EL_FLOW): an EXECUTE only holds a date if its chain is 100% closed (SOW · signed WO · sub confirmed · material with ETA · Before inspection). Practical form: if an EXECUTE has a date this week at the 2:30 meeting, its previous tasks must be at 100% — otherwise the EXECUTE moves, it is not left "just in case".
- Collections (EL_FLOW Fase E): trade closed + documented → REQUEST BANK PAYMENT → RECEIVE BANK PAYMENT — "this is how each finished trade becomes a collection." The CEO manages the draw; the COO guarantees the work side is truly closed (After checklist, city inspection passed) so the draw is requestable.
- Milestone movement (Next Week gerencia report): per project — which collection is next, HOW MUCH, WHEN, chip "SE COBRA ESTA SEMANA", and "how this week brings it closer" built only from tasks the schedule itself chains to the collection. Real chain example (Martha): framing inspection → siding → siding+drywall collection $44k.
- Inspections are collection gates: a red tag does not just delay a trade — it delays the draw behind it. Quality = money.

Real case: case #3 — Martha's red tags (24-jul): inspections rejected for wrong sequence requested and plans not present on site — the failure that had already been predicted. The COO's site verification (sequence + approved plans physically on site) is exactly what prevents it. Complementary: case #8 — material crossed between jobs (César using Martha's material elsewhere), detected by chat, not by system: the COO is the pair of eyes on site.

Exercise: on a real project, map its next collection milestone: which tasks chain to it, which are at risk this week, what you verify on site tomorrow so the inspection passes FIRST time. Write the 3-line escalation if one chain task has no closed predecessor.

Evaluated: for any active project, names its next milestone (amount + blocking chain) without opening the budget; zero EXECUTEs allowed through the 2:30 meeting with an open chain in his visited projects.

MODULE E5 · Measuring the team — outputs, Friday vs Friday, recognize

Objective: measure the operation's people with the GUIA_CEO method: observable outputs, honest context, recognition — never sensations.

Content:
- The 3 rules (GUIA_CEO §1): measure outputs observable in the systems (inspection passed, payment out, photos in CC, task closed in JT) — not hours or feelings · the systems already exist — look at them with fixed criteria daily · measure to RECOGNIZE, not to hunt culprits.
- The COO's lens vs the CEO's: the CEO runs the closing ritual and the semáforo; the COO reads the same fichas for the OPERATIONAL side — which PM's field execution needs his presence, which "red" is actually a system lie (Giselle's corrupt tasks.json; Jeff's forecast being more honest than the schedule).
- Friday vs Friday is law (C2): evaluation = what got CLOSED between Friday baselines; reschedules in neutral; "vencidas" = a cleanup list, not a verdict.
- The advisor's warnings (GUIA_CEO §6), the 4 the COO most needs: activity ≠ result (Jeff: 15 days silent yet invoiced the month's biggest milestone $82,780) · the one who chats most is not the one who produces most · data punishes those who work without registering — one question before any conclusion ("real, or stale data?") · the example comes from the top — the worst July numbers were the owners'.
- His own row in the weekly semáforo: payments not overdue/handed off · COO entries in bitácoras · ≥1 signal/day. He is measured with the same honesty he applies.
- Recognition mechanics (GUIA_CEO §4): same-day, specific, with the data — "Rey — vi tus fotos de [obra] y la nota de calidad en la bitácora. Así se ve el rol de COO. Cumplido." When not met: question, not reproach (¿qué te bloqueó? + ¿qué necesitas? + ¿para cuándo?).

Real case: the Jeff paradox (GUIA_CEO §6.1): red across the dashboard, silent 15 days — and still closed the biggest invoice of the month with a forecast better than the system's. A COO who reads only the semáforo punishes his best closer; a COO who asks "what did you CLOSE this week?" first, directs.

Exercise: take the latest Project Dept Weekly + one PM's weekly. Produce: 1 specific recognition message (with the data), 1 question-not-reproach for an unmet check, and 1 "red" you verified as real-vs-stale before concluding.

Evaluated: his weekly reads produce recognition + questions (never day-by-day blame); zero conclusions on a new red without the one verification question; his own Día Cumplido sustained — certification = N consecutive days with the 4 checks ✅ [PROPUESTA: N=10 hábiles, mismo criterio que Track D — VERIFICAR CON JOSE].
