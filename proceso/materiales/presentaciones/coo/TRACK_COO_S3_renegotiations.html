COO Track · Session 3 — Renegotiations: With Details, Always Notified

Track COO · Rey Direction module · 30–45 min Sources: canon §6/§8 · EL_FLOW
The rule (canon §8, P3): if CEO/COO renegotiates a price already closed by the PM (e.g. $1,000 → $800), he MUST notify the change so the Work Order is updated and the company does not overpay. A renegotiation that never reached the system = money overpaid, a blind cashflow, and a dirty record.

1 · Objective

This section exists so you know what "done" looks like. Run every negotiation so the cashflow SEES it: terms with details, WO updated same day, payments requested only through the gatekeeper.

2 · Content — the COO's money lane

His three moves in the money flow (canon §8)

  • New Negotiations WITH DETAILS — amounts, dates, conditions → they feed the CASHFLOW DATA (with the Administrator's Friday balances + the Backoffice's Daily Schedule Update) → CASHFLOW PLAN → the CEO approves.
  • Request Payments — go to the Administrator, who runs the 4 filters: in the week's plan? · same amount? · leaves us in good position? · documentation = SIGNED WO + inspections. No signed WO → the request does not pass, and should not.
  • Update Schedule — a payment that executes and unblocks work updates the schedule, so the theory matches reality again.

Money rules he negotiates WITHIN

  • Subs are never paid up front ("se van y te dejan varado" — Jose). Standard payment = the Friday after finishing.
  • Downpayment = exception for CERTAIN subs/trades — framing, MEP rough-ins — with its gate (the only pre-work payment).
  • The payment task's original date = contractual trigger (completion + terms). It is never rewritten; the payment MOVES and is recorded on the real pay date.
  • ALL money passes through the schedule (canon §6, rule without exceptions) — even last-minute buys and petty cash (Additional Expense). Money moved without a task = cashflow, productivity and reports go blind.

3 · The real case

Case #6 — the WO is the negotiation, in writing: a sub claimed his payment 1 day after finishing; his signed WO said "1 week after" — and by contract, he waited ("no es como acuerdo de palabra"). The WO's terms ARE the negotiation. Corollary: renegotiate and don't update the WO, and the old paper wins — the payment goes out at the old amount.
Case #7 — Felton, 17-jul: a bounced client check conditioning whether the sub works Saturday. When money touches execution, the COO/PM escalates to the Administrator and CEO THAT day — nobody discovers it on Saturday.

4 · The exercise

Simulation on a real project (Karim or Martha): you renegotiated a closed price down. Write the 3-line notification: old price → new price · which WO gets updated · what the cashflow gains. Then take your LAST real Request Payment and run the 4 filters on it out loud — verdict: passes / moves with reason / returned for missing file.

5 · What gets evaluated

  • Zero renegotiations without same-day notification (the WO always ends up matching the deal).
  • Every negotiation delivered WITH details: amount, date, terms — never "I got a better price" without numbers.
  • Zero payment requests without their signed WO; money-touching-execution issues escalated the same day.
